## What This Does

My community is houseplant owners: the advice threads and questions where people trade tips on keeping plants alive. My classifier reads a single post or comment and sorts it by the care topic it is mainly about. There are four topics: `plant_selection`, `watering_soil`, `pest_management` and `general_care`. The point is to route a question to the people and answers that fit it, such as sending "why are my leaves yellow and the soil damp" to watering advice rather than to a general pile.

---

## Label Taxonomy

### `plant_selection`

**Definition:** Recommends which plants to get, for a new owner or for a home with pets.

**Example 1:**
> Pothos! They are great for beginners... they don't need a ton of light, so they're good indoors.

**Example 2:**
> If you have pets, make sure what you're getting isn't toxic :)

### `watering_soil`

**Definition:** About how much, how often or by what method to water, or about potting mix, pot size, drainage and repotting.

**Example 1:**
> Watering on a schedule rather than checking the soil, and not knowing that different plants have different watering requirements

**Example 2:**
> Immediately repot everything into huge pots when I bought them. So many things died that way

### `pest_management`

**Definition:** About spotting, preventing or treating pests, including quarantining new plants.

**Example 1:**
> I used to think quarantining new plants wasn't all that important... and helllooo thrips. Goodbyyyye lots of plants.

**Example 2:**
> Beneficial nematodes... that combined with the yellow sticky traps seems to have handled it.

### `general_care`

**Definition:** All other upkeep: light and placement, propagation, fertilizing, cleaning, inspections, seasonal changes, and the habit or mindset of caring for plants.

**Example 1:**
> Light is more important than you think. You should be able to read small print without turning a light on.

**Example 2:**
> Most of them have slow release fertilizer so I don't have to worry about that.

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `watering_soil` and `general_care`. Water shows up inside almost every routine and every light or placement problem, so it is easy to pull a post toward `watering_soil`.

**The borderline post:**
> A lot of the typical not enough light/too much water set ups we see asking for help on here were me for my first year or two. I didn't realize how little light I had

**The decision rule I used every time:** If the post is about how, when or how much to water, or about the pot, soil, drainage or repotting, it's `watering_soil`. If water is only one thing mentioned in a routine, or only a symptom of something else such as low light, it's `general_care`. The borderline post above is `general_care`, because its point is that the author underestimated their light.

**A second rule, for `pest_management` and `watering_soil`:** If the post is about a pest, spotting it, treating it or keeping it from spreading, it's `pest_management`, even when the pest is in the soil or the fix involves repotting. If a pest is only a passing sign and the question asked is about watering or the pot, it's `watering_soil`.

| F1 — `watering_soil` | 0.462 | 0.000 | -0.462 |

**What I predicted before I looked:**

My trained model will beat the baseline most on `general_care`, since that label is a catch-all ("all other upkeep") that's hard to define in one sentence for a zero-shot model but should be learnable from labeled examples; I expect the smallest gap on `pest_management`, where the baseline already scores well (F1 0.857) because that label's definition is narrow and distinctive.

**What the gap actually means:**

The prediction was wrong, and not by a little: the trained model lost to the zero-shot baseline on every measure, not just `general_care`. The confusion matrix in `results.json` shows why — the trained model predicted `pest_management` for all 30 held-out posts, regardless of their true label. It didn't learn a weaker version of the task; it learned to output one class and ignore the input. That's why `general_care`, `watering_soil`, and `plant_selection` all show F1 0.000 for the trained model (it never once predicted them), and why its `pest_management` F1 (0.421) is actually worse than the baseline's (0.857) — it's right every time pest_management is the true label (recall 1.0) but wrong the rest of the time (precision 0.267), since it never predicts anything else. Fine-tuning here didn't add nothing — it made things worse than having no model at all. That points to a training problem (likely a dataset/class-balance or optimization issue) to dig into before trusting this model, not a case where the baseline and trained model were simply comparable.

**Diagnoses**

**Criteria 1 and 2 (accuracy, per-label F1): mostly a model problem, with a possible labelling problem underneath.**
The confusion matrix for seed 42 is a single column: all 30 test posts were called `pest_management`. A model that ignores its input and outputs one label is not showing me a boundary I labelled badly. It is a model that has not learned anything yet. The training log agrees: loss only went from about 1.38 to 1.34, and 1.386 is ln(4), which is what a four-way coin flip scores. With 200 posts, about 140 of them in training, batch size 16 and 3 epochs, the model takes roughly 27 gradient steps at a learning rate of 2e-5. That is very little training for a model that starts with a randomly initialised classification head. I think this is the main cause, but I have not tested it yet.

The agreement report is what stops me from blaming the model for everything. I agreed with staff on only 18 of 30 posts (60%), and 9 of my 12 disagreements were pile (a): the written rule gave a clear answer and I applied it loosely. That shows I do not apply written rules consistently when I am labelling. The caveat is that those 30 posts were labelled under the staff taxonomy (`analysis`, `hot_take`, `reaction`), not my plant taxonomy, so the report measures my habits, not the 200 plant labels directly. It does not tell me how many of my 200 labels are wrong. It does tell me I should expect some, most likely at the `watering_soil` / `general_care` boundary, where I wrote a decision rule and where my 0.55 F1 target depends on the rule being applied the same way each time.

`plant_selection` at 0.000 F1 in all three seeds is the one result I cannot put on a collapse. In seed 7 it was never predicted, and its biggest error was 5 real `plant_selection` posts called `general_care`. Some of my `plant_selection` posts are short plant recommendations ("ZZ plant is also easy.") that mention ease of care, which is also what `general_care` is about. That is a genuinely hard label pair, and it may be a labelling-consistency problem as well. I would need to re-read those posts to know.

**Criterion 4 (seed spread): a consequence of the same weak model.**
A 0.167 spread comes from a model that is barely above chance, so which labels it happens to favour changes with the split. Test sets of 30 posts also make each post worth 0.033 accuracy, so a spread of 0.10 is only three posts. I do not expect this to reach 0.10 until the model is learning something stable.

**Criterion 5 (confidence): a model problem, seen in one seed.**
In seed 42 the model gave the same label to every post, so its confidence told me nothing about which predictions were right (0.30 vs 0.30). That is the collapse again. Seeds 7 and 2024 passed, so confidence is informative when the model has learned something. This is a miss that should go away if the training problem is fixed, and I will check that in the after run.

## The Improvement

**What I changed:**
One training setting: `EPOCHS` from 3 to 10. Nothing else changed. Learning rate (2e-5), batch size (16), `MAX_LENGTH` (128), the base model (`distilbert-base-uncased`), the 200 labels and the three seeds (42, 7, 2024) are the same as in the before run. Both runs were on Apple Silicon (`mps`, torch 2.14.1). I ran the three seeds with `RUN_LABEL = "after"`, so the result is in `results_three_seeds_after.json` and the before file is untouched.

**Which diagnosis pointed at it:**
My diagnosis for criteria 1 and 2 was undertraining: about 139 training posts at batch size 16 is 9 steps an epoch, so 3 epochs was about 27 steps, and the before run collapsed to one label with loss stuck near 1.38 (chance for four labels). 10 epochs is about 90 steps. I picked this over relabelling because the collapse is not something a relabel can explain. A model that outputs one label for every post has not learned any boundary, mine or anyone else's.

## What's Still Broken

**Criterion 1 (accuracy ≥ 0.70, all seeds):** still missed, in seed 2024 (0.667). **Criterion 2 (every label F1 ≥ 0.55, all seeds):** still missed, in seed 2024, where `general_care` is 0.500 and `watering_soil` is 0.545. The matrix above shows the pair: 4 of 7 real `watering_soil` posts were called `general_care` and none went the other way. In seeds 7 and 2024, 3 of 7 real `plant_selection` posts were called `general_care` too. So `general_care` is absorbing posts from both neighbours, which fits it being my catch-all label ("all other upkeep") and the one where my water-versus-light-and-routine rule is hardest to apply the same way every time. My agreement report found I apply written rules loosely (9 of 12 disagreements), so I would re-read the `general_care`, `watering_soil` and `plant_selection` posts against my own rules and count how many rows change before retraining. **Criterion 4 (seed spread ≤ 0.10):** met, but with no margin. The spread is exactly 0.100, so one post in a different seed would fail it. I would not call this solid.

**The gap between what I meant my labels to capture and what the model learned:**
I meant `general_care` to be everything left over, but the model treats it as the default answer: in seed 2024 it takes 3 of 7 `plant_selection` posts and 4 of 7 `watering_soil` posts, and almost nothing is mistaken for it in the other direction. My other labels have clearer cues (pest names, plant names) than the catch-all does, so the model seems to have learned the cues and fallen back on `general_care` when it saw none.
